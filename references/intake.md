# Intake — the six questions

Run these before touching Clay. The answers determine whether to build at
all, and then populate the spec.

## 1. Offer type — readiness or manufacturable?

**Readiness:** the buyer decides when something in their world changes.
They didn't want it last month; something happened and now they do.
Vanta is the clean case — you buy SOC 2 automation when an enterprise
deal stalls in procurement. No copy makes a company want SOC 2 before
that moment arrives.

**Manufacturable:** the want can be created by the pitch itself. Show a
founder a Clay build that triples reply rate and they want it, regardless
of what happened at their company this quarter.

Detection is expensive — credits, Claygent calls, a six-layer build,
maintenance as sources break. That cost is only worth paying when timing
is the binding constraint.

**If manufacturable: stop.** Sell ICP definition and messaging instead.
A window build here is weeks of work solving a problem that isn't the
bottleneck.

Treat it as a spectrum. Ask where the majority of buying energy sits.
Manufacturable offers still benefit from *fit* filtering — that's cheap.
It's the time-decaying window layer you skip.

## 2. What is the buying event?

Ask: **what happened in the buyer's world in the two weeks before each of
your last five closed deals?**

Good answer — specific, recurring, dated: "they hired a Controller",
"their inference bill crossed a threshold", "an enterprise prospect sent
a security questionnaire".

Bad answer — vague or invented: "they realized they needed to modernize",
"they saw our content". That's a manufacturable offer wearing a costume.

If the client cannot answer this from actual closed deals, you do not
have a window build yet. Send them to look.

## 3. Where is the signal surface?

Is the event visible from outside the company, and specifically where?

- job posts (content, not just volume)
- GitHub / Hugging Face activity
- engineering blog, docs, changelog
- funding and press announcements
- partner pages, case studies, customer logos
- review sites (G2, Capterra) — including complaints
- tech-stack detection
- regulatory filings

**If the event is invisible from outside: stop.** Payroll frustration,
internal tooling pain, a souring vendor relationship — real triggers, no
public trace. Weak proxies produce confident, wrong rationales at scale.

## 4. How long is the window?

Days, weeks, or months?

Under two weeks and your enrichment cadence loses — by the time the row
scores, the decision is made. That's a different machine: contract intel,
review-site monitoring, or just asking.

## 5. Who can never buy? (→ becomes L0)

Push hard here. The client will name obvious competitors. You want the
accounts that look like perfect fits and can't convert:

- **Competing supply** — they sell the thing
- **Locked in** — multi-year commitment, co-selling deal, entrenched
  enterprise agreement
- **Self-serving** — they built it internally, or run their own fleet
- **Wrong size** — below the budget floor, or above the procurement moat
- **Wrong shape** — services business with no product surface

## 6. What do they buy instead? (→ becomes the penalty)

Name the substitutes and ask what each looks like publicly. A case study,
logo placement, or press mention with a substitute in the recent past is
strong evidence the window already closed.

---

## Output mapping

- Q1 and Q4 → build or don't build
- Q2 and Q3 → L2 Tier A signals
- Q5 → L0 disqualification checks
- Q6 → the penalty column
